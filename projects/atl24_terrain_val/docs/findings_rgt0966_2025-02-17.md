# Findings: ATL24 terrain vs ATL08 ground vs 3DEP, RGT 0966 cycle 26 (2025-02-17)

One granule, six beams, US part (32.6–49.0°N, NV / eastern CA / OR / ID / WA).
Reference: USGS 3DEP 1 m DTM, 372 corridor tiles from 26 lidar projects.
Segments: ATL03 20 m geosegments (`segment_id`), the same for both classifiers.
dz = photon height − 3DEP, both in NAD83(2011) + NAVD88.
"Paired" = segments where both classifiers have ≥ 5 terrain photons: 124,181
segments, 10,632 of them in the mountain subset (DEM ≥ 1500 m, slope ≥ 10°).
About half of the paired segments are flagged as snow by ATL08 (IMS). Snow
depth is **not** removed.

Run: `build_photons.py → stage_3dep.py → build_segments.py → analyze.py`.
Tables and figures are in `outputs/results/`.

## Checks

- **ATL24 CSV alignment**: every ATL24 CSV row equals the matching ATL03
  photon (time < 1e-6 s, h < 2e-4 m), for all six beams. `atl24_prob_0..4`
  map to classes 0, 1, 2, 40, 41 (the argmax matches `class_ph` for 98.5–99.3%
  of photons).
- **Datum**: on flat ground (< 3°, paired), the median all-signal dz is
  −0.01 m and the ATL08 ground dz is −0.02 m. The ITRF2014 → NAD83(2011) +
  GEOID18 chain is right.
- **Per-photon vs segment-centre dz** agree within about 0.05 m up to 20°
  (`method_compare.csv`). Above 25° the segment-centre method reads 0.1–0.5 m
  lower for ATL08.

## Results

**1. Shean's ATL08 result reproduces.** Median dz by slope, paired segments:

| slope | all signal | ATL08 ground | ATL24 terrain | ATL08 h_te_best_fit_20m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 3° | −0.01 | −0.02 | −0.01 | |
| 17.5–22.5° | +0.09 | −0.48 to −0.72 | −0.18 to −0.26 | |
| 30–35° | −0.2 to −0.3 | −1.8 to −2.1 | −0.5 | |

Shean's metric (median at 17.5–22.5° minus median below 3°):

| estimator | value |
|---|---|
| ATL08 ground photons | **−0.56 m** |
| ATL08 `h_te_best_fit_20m` | −0.60 m |
| ATL24 terrain | **−0.20 m** |
| all signal | +0.10 m |

The snow-free paired subset gives ATL08 −1.97 m and ATL24 −0.64 m, but it has
only 649 segments at 20°, most of them in southern CA / NV. That is close to
Shean's 2025 open-terrain value (about −1.7 to −1.9 m). On snow-covered
segments the measured bias is smaller, which fits snow depth offsetting part
of it.

**2. ATL24 terrain is much less biased on slopes, but mainly because it
selects almost all of the surface return.** Share of signal photons labelled
terrain, median per segment:

| slope | ATL24 | ATL08 |
|---|---|---|
| < 3° | 100% | 95% |
| 17.5–22.5° | 92% | 19% |
| > 30° | 90% | 12% |

So ATL24 terrain behaves almost like the all-signal estimator:
- its median per-segment spread (MAD) is 1.33 m at 20°, against 1.55 m for all
  signal and 0.31 m for ATL08;
- the share of its photons below the signal peak stays at 0.55–0.57, against
  0.70–0.77 for ATL08 (`fig_below_peak.png`).

ATL08 follows the lower edge of the widened return, as Shean describes.
`fig_profile_1.png` shows this at 35°: ATL08 ground sits 2–8 m below the DTM,
while ATL24 terrain covers the whole ±8 m return. ATL24 is a better *median*
estimator on slopes, not a sharper ground finder.

**3. Error metrics.** Paired segments:

| | ATL08 all | ATL08 mountain | ATL24 all | ATL24 mountain | all signal all | all signal mountain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bias (median) | −0.04 | −0.41 | −0.02 | −0.10 | −0.01 | +0.07 |
| MAE | 0.30 | 1.02 | 0.23 | 0.80 | 0.22 | 0.68 |
| RMSE | 0.69 | 1.52 | 0.75 | 2.17 | 0.47 | 0.95 |
| NMAD | 0.18 | 0.97 | 0.16 | 0.81 | 0.16 | 0.78 |

ATL24's higher RMSE comes from gross errors (next point).

**4. ATL24 has rare gross errors.** 174 of 168,319 segments (0.10%) where
ATL24 has ≥ 5 terrain photons are off by more than 10 m, up to +1,465 m.
93% of them are segments where ATL08 found no ground, so likely clouds or
noise labelled as terrain. These push the RMSE on ATL24's own coverage to
8.2 m (NMAD 0.17 m). ATL08 has 25 such segments (0.02%).

**5. Coverage on steep slopes is poor for both, worse for ATL24.**
Segments with a signal return (≥ 5 photons) where each classifier has ≥ 5
terrain photons (`fig_coverage.png`):
- below 7.5°, ATL24 alone fills 20–28% of segments that ATL08 misses;
- above 12.5° this reverses: ATL08-only is 16–22%, ATL24-only 4–14%;
- above 20°, more than half of the segments have neither.

The paired comparison therefore uses the steep segments where both classifiers
work, which is a survivorship sample.

**6. Slope orientation (slope ≥ 10°).** ATL08 reads −0.57 m when the slope
falls along the track and −0.43 m across it. ATL24 reads −0.13 and −0.20 m.
This is one granule, so it can't separate orientation from location.

**7. Official ATL08 100 m heights** (`h_te_best_fit`, `h_te_median`,
`h_te_interp`) applied to 20 m segments have NMAD 0.7 m overall and 5 m in the
mountains. That reflects 100 m vs 20 m sampling, not a classifier difference.
Compare those values at 100 m before quoting them.

## Caveats

- One winter granule: snow depth is in every snow-flagged dz, and the result
  covers one date and one geometry.
- 3DEP projects span 2015–2024, so terrain may have changed since the survey.
  The newest project is used where tiles overlap.
- `signal_conf_ph[:,0] >= 4` defines "all signal". Shean used SlideRule's
  signal_class with ATL06-SR fits on 40 m segments; we use medians on 20 m
  geosegments.
